PicoBridge

Lets my desk Windows keyboard and mouse switch smoothly to phones and a second PC while carrying clipboard and files along. Grew from an ESP32-S3 hardware bridge into a native Windows Bluetooth HID driver, desktop app, and Android companion built for daily switching.

Daily UsePrivate
WhyWhat I builtEngineeringRole & AITrade-offsOutcome

Why

Why I built it

I wanted my desk keyboard and mouse on my phone and fresh phone clipboard items back on the PC. Getting keystrokes through is the easy part; idle Bluetooth interference, clipboard ownership, and update survival are what make or break it.

What I built

What I built

  • A C# desktop app, a test-signed Windows driver in C, a Java Android app, and a PowerShell build, sign, and release pipeline.
  • Single offline installer with in-place update and rollback, tested daily on a Galaxy S23 and a second PC.
  • Evolved from an ESP32-S3 hardware bridge handling three Bluetooth hosts plus USB mic and speaker.

Engineering

Problems I worked through

  • Even while idle, phone Bluetooth audio stuttered; HCI traces pointed to keyboard LED output reports, and fixing the descriptor kept standby alive while restoring clean playback.
  • Clipboard ownership separates PC-originated content from items newly copied on the phone so echoed data never overwrites desktop work.
  • Desktop flicker initially looked like an animation glitch until frame captures caught a hidden list flashing during sync; fixing visibility eliminated it.
  • Windows and Android updates keep pairing identities, settings, and a rollback path so fixes never force a full re-pair.

Role & AI

How I split the work with AI

I own the switching ergonomics and stability bar through daily use across Windows, a Galaxy phone, and a second PC, collaborating with Codex and other agents across the C driver, C# desktop app, and Android client.

Trade-offs

What testing made me change

  • Input rides Bluetooth Classic HID while the merged Android app syncs clipboard and files over LAN; both channels report status and reconnect independently for clear troubleshooting.
  • The custom Windows driver uses test-signing alongside a single offline installer and rollback script, tuned for reliable daily switching across my Windows machines.

Outcome

Outcome

In daily use across Windows, phone, and a second PC covering input switching, audio coexistence, and clipboard handoff, with rollback and two-device verification on updates.
