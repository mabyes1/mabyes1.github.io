DrumLab & music-sep

My own drum-practice workstation: Demucs splits stems, ADTOF transcribes drums to MIDI, and a browser editor lets me record my e-drum kit bar by bar and export standard MIDI.

Why

Why I built it

Drum practice requires clean stems, a log of what I actually played, and a click that breathes with natural tempo drift—raw model beat predictions alone are far from a usable practice tool.

What I built

What I built

  • music-sep CLI: Demucs (htdemucs_ft, CUDA) stem separation and ADTOF drum transcription.
  • DrumLab: 20–55 ms edits on a 96-bar chart; localhost-only with Host/Origin checks on every write and raw hits saved as an audit trail.
  • sticky-click: Beat This! downbeats smoothed by a tempo-continuity tracker that ignores fill spikes, giving a click track that follows live playing.

Role & AI

How I split the work with AI

I test every change myself behind the e-drum kit, listening for groove alignment before iterating on stems, notation, and practice flow with coding agents.

Trade-offs

What testing made me change

  • Pretrained models handle separation and transcription; the real work is shaping their output into charts you can actually practice with.
  • Drum fills trigger false downbeat spikes, so the adaptive click enforces bar-level continuity—always verified by listening through full songs.

Outcome

Outcome

Powers my personal stem, notation, and MIDI workflow, with the adaptive click kept as a separate listening-tested experiment.
